The gist of my work

My PhD followed the machine learning pipeline from the data people create, through the models that data shapes, to the model decisions people experience. At Meta I build that pipeline itself, for real users, from ranking what people see to the data models learn from.

The loop

My experience at Meta and in my Ph.D. can be summarized by this loop.

People create data. A model learns from it and makes decisions about people. People react to the decisions made about them, and their reactions are collected as data and used to update the model again, so the cycle starts again.

Theme · Crowdsourcing — labels from many people

Accounting for Confirmation Bias in Crowdsourced Label Aggregation

Meric Altug Gemalmaz and Ming Yin

Quick recap: Labels gathered from many people carry each person's confirmation bias — the tendency to favor what they already believe. This paper's model estimates each annotator's bias and corrects for it as the labels are combined, so the combined labels end up substantially less biased.

The 30th International Joint Conference on Artificial Intelligence (IJCAI), Online, August 2021.

Themes · Fairness · People and AI

Understanding Decision Subjects' Fairness Perceptions and Retention in Repeated Interactions with AI-Based Decision Systems

Meric Altug Gemalmaz and Ming Yin

Quick recap: When people face an AI's decisions repeatedly — here, loan decisions — their fairness judgments, and whether they stay or leave, depend more on whether the system favors their own group than on whether it treats every group the same. Fairness, for the people a system decides about, is judged from where their own group stands.

The 5th AAAI/ACM Conference on AI, Ethics, and Society (AIES), Oxford, UK, August 2022.

Themes · Fairness · People and AI

Understanding Decision Subjects' Engagement with and Perceived Fairness of AI Models When Opportunities of Qualification Improvement Exist

Meric Altug Gemalmaz and Ming Yin

Quick recap: In three experiments, when people had a real opportunity to improve their qualifications, an AI's unfairness did not reduce their engagement or their effort to improve. It did not soften their judgment either: when the AI treated their own group worse than the others, people still saw the system as less fair.

Themes · Fairness · People and AI

An Investigation of Decision Subjects' Interaction With Periodically Updated ML-Based Task Allocations

Meric Altug Gemalmaz and Ming Yin

Quick recap: When an AI assigns tasks and keeps updating those assignments, people are not reacting to one decision but to a moving series of them. This chapter studies how people respond to periodically updated task allocations over time, and how their sense of the system's fairness forms as the decisions change.
